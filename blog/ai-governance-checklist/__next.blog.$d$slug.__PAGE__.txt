a:T22c1,<p>An enterprise AI governance checklist is the tool a company uses, when adopting several AI services at once, to verify that key issuance, model permissions, data boundaries, and budget attribution are actually in place — written for platform teams, security and legal, and anyone who signs off on the AI bill.</p>
<p>The conclusion first: the unit of governance is the <a class="doc-term" href="/en/docs/resources" data-def="The unit that permissions and billing hang off — keys, allowed models, and credit budget all belong to one.">project</a>. Keys hang off projects, budgets hang off projects, and every request record hangs off projects — get <a href="/en/blog/one-project-one-key">one project, one key</a> right and half of the 12 checks below pass automatically. The checks come in four groups, ordered the way real adoption goes: stop the bleeding first, then put things on rails. If you want the billing side first, pair this with <a href="/en/blog/how-to-read-your-ai-bill">the three-layer guide to reading your AI bill</a>.</p>
<h2 id="group-one-keys-and-identity">Group one: keys and identity</h2>
<h3 id="1-one-project-one-key-keys-follow-projects-not-people">1. One project, one key — keys follow projects, not people</h3>
<p>When someone leaves or changes teams, you revoke one project&#39;s access; you don&#39;t rotate a key the whole company shares. A shared key is the most expensive technical debt in governance: when something goes wrong you know &quot;someone,&quot; never &quot;who.&quot;</p>
<h3 id="2-show-keys-once-store-them-centrally-revocable-and-traceable">2. Show keys once, store them centrally — revocable and traceable</h3>
<p>Any key you issue must be revocable at any moment, and after revoking it you should be able to check the request log for what it served and how large the blast radius was.</p>
<h3 id="3-separate-production-from-experiments-test-quotas-cheap-enough-to-burn">3. Separate production from experiments — test quotas cheap enough to burn</h3>
<p>A test key&#39;s cap should be low enough that misuse never becomes a month-end surprise. Production keys go through issuance with an approval trail.</p>
<h2 id="group-two-models-and-data-boundaries">Group two: models and data boundaries</h2>
<h3 id="4-maintain-a-model-allowlist-switching-models-is-a-decision-not-a-code-change">4. Maintain a model allowlist — switching models is a decision, not a code change</h3>
<p>Not every project needs the most capable model. An allowlist turns &quot;switching models&quot; into a decision that requires a reason; see the <a href="/en/docs/models">model catalog</a> for what is available and how each is priced.</p>
<h3 id="5-classify-your-data-engineers-should-not-decide-case-by-case">5. Classify your data — engineers should not decide case by case</h3>
<p>Which fields may go to an external model, and which must be de-identified first? Write it on one page. For a reference structure, map it against the <a href="https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NIST AI Risk Management Framework</a>.</p>
<h3 id="6-record-each-vendor-39-s-data-retention-policy-in-one-document">6. Record each vendor&#39;s data retention policy — in one document</h3>
<p>When legal or security asks, the answer should not live in someone&#39;s inbox.</p>
<h2 id="group-three-budgets-and-attribution">Group three: budgets and attribution</h2>
<h3 id="7-set-spending-caps-at-the-project-level-alert-first-cut-off-second">7. Set spending caps at the project level — alert first, cut off second</h3>
<p>The first notice of an overrun should never be the invoice.</p>
<h3 id="8-keep-per-request-records-answer-quot-who-spent-this-and-on-what-quot">8. Keep per-request records — answer &quot;who spent this, and on what&quot;</h3>
<p>Every call maps to a project, key, model, and token counts; that is what makes spend auditable. Field details are in the <a href="/en/docs/spend">spend docs</a>.</p>
<h3 id="9-reconcile-monthly-in-one-unit-watch-trends-not-just-totals">9. Reconcile monthly in one unit — watch trends, not just totals</h3>
<p>Convert every vendor&#39;s usage into the same unit (for example <a href="/en/docs/credits" class="doc-term-def" data-def="The billing unit. 1 USD = 100 credits; usage converts at each model's list rate.">credits</a>); trends surface problems weeks before absolute numbers do.</p>
<p><a href="/en/docs/credits" class="doc-term-def" data-def="The billing unit. 1 USD = 100 credits; usage converts at each model's list rate.">See how credits are priced →</a></p>
<h2 id="group-four-process-and-audit">Group four: process and audit</h2>
<h3 id="10-standardize-onboarding-of-new-services-one-more-service-one-more-row-of-config">10. Standardize onboarding of new services — one more service, one more row of config</h3>
<p>Evaluation, allowlist, key, budget — all four before go-live, instead of whoever opens an account first.</p>
<h3 id="11-write-an-incident-runbook-know-what-to-shut-off-when-a-key-leaks">11. Write an incident runbook — know what to shut off when a key leaks</h3>
<p>Revoke the key, trace the log, reissue — three steps, written down and rehearsed once.</p>
<h3 id="12-quarterly-inventory-and-permission-cleanup-governance-is-a-cycle-not-a-one-off">12. Quarterly inventory and permission cleanup — governance is a cycle, not a one-off</h3>
<p>Every quarter: which projects are still alive, which keys have had no traffic for thirty days, which permissions can be reclaimed.</p>
<h2 id="applying-the-checklist-by-company-size">Applying the checklist by company size</h2>
<h3 id="under-10-people-start-with-checks-1-7-and-8">Under 10 people: start with checks 1, 7, and 8</h3>
<p>A small team needs no committee — project keys, spending caps, and per-request records are all configuration, not process.</p>
<h3 id="50-to-200-people-add-the-allowlist-and-data-classification">50 to 200 people: add the allowlist and data classification</h3>
<p>Cross-team usage starts to diverge; checks 4 and 5 make &quot;who may use which model&quot; answerable.</p>
<h3 id="500-and-up-turn-on-process-and-audit">500 and up: turn on process and audit</h3>
<p>Checks 10 through 12 become the center of gravity — governance graduates from settings to a recurring institution inside internal audit scope.</p>
<h2 id="three-adoption-scenarios-by-industry">Three adoption scenarios by industry</h2>
<h3 id="software-and-internet-speed-first-govern-by-defaults">Software and internet: speed first, govern by defaults</h3>
<p>Low test quotas plus a production allowlist keep experiments fast and launches safe.</p>
<h3 id="finance-and-other-compliance-heavy-industries-data-boundaries-first">Finance and other compliance-heavy industries: data boundaries first</h3>
<p>Max out checks 5 and 6 before widening model access; per-request auditability is the shared language of internal and external auditors.</p>
<h3 id="manufacturing-and-customer-support-floors-attribution-decides-the-budget">Manufacturing and customer support floors: attribution decides the budget</h3>
<p>High-volume repetitive requests make cost attribution decisive — checks 8 and 9 determine whether next year&#39;s AI budget survives.</p>
<h2 id="the-more-modern-approach-turn-the-checklist-into-system-defaults">The more modern approach: turn the checklist into system defaults</h2>
<p>The traditional way to enforce these 12 checks is a policy document and human diligence; the more modern way is a governance platform that ships them as defaults. On ATP Token, the <a class="doc-term" href="/en/docs/console-setup" data-def="The top level of an account. Workspaces and projects sit underneath it.">organization</a> → workspace → project hierarchy is check 1 by construction; project keys carry allowlists and quotas, covering checks 4 and 7; every request writes its own record, so checks 8 and 9 need nobody&#39;s memory. The interface is compatible with the OpenAI, Anthropic, and Gemini formats — integration is a base_url and a key.</p>
