<h2 id="faqs">FAQs</h2>
<h3 id="how-does-token-based-ai-pricing-differ-from-seat-based-saas">How does token-based AI pricing differ from seat-based SaaS?</h3>
<p>Seat pricing scales with named users. Token pricing scales with usage volume and shape—prompt size, output length, retries, and agent steps—so cost can spike without hiring.</p>
<h3 id="what-metrics-replace-cost-per-seat-for-ai">What metrics replace cost per seat for AI?</h3>
<p>Credits consumed vs allocated by project, average cost per thousand requests, and cost per completed agent task. Monthly totals alone cannot drive decisions.</p>
<h3 id="how-should-departments-budget-for-ai-in-2026">How should departments budget for AI in 2026?</h3>
<p>Give each product or BU a project allocation in a shared settlement unit, review burn weekly, and keep experimental sandboxes on separate low caps.</p>
<h3 id="why-do-ai-features-inside-saas-products-complicate-erp-budgets">Why do AI features inside SaaS products complicate ERP budgets?</h3>
<p>Vendors add usage meters—tokens, AI actions, document processing—on top of seats. Without caps and reporting rights, adoption growth becomes an unplanned line item.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-first-step-to-modernize-ai-budgeting">What is the first step to modernize AI budgeting?</h3>
<p>Inventory all AI usage paths and keys, pick one settlement unit, assign project owners, and put enforceable allocations on production workloads.</p>
