25:T1997,<p>An AI spending cap is a limit on how much model usage a <a class="doc-term" href="/en/docs/resources" data-def="The unit that permissions and billing hang off — keys, allowed models, and credit budget all belong to one.">project</a> or team may consume in a period—enforced as an allocation or hard stop, not only as a slide in a budget deck. This guide is for platform and finance teams who need ceilings that survive go-live.</p>
<p>Answer first: <strong>the allocation is the cap.</strong> Push credits down <a class="doc-term" href="/en/docs/console-setup" data-def="The top level of an account. Workspaces and projects sit underneath it.">organization</a> → workspace → project, alert before exhaustion, and isolate experiments. Unlimited cards plus “please don’t overspend” is how public overrun stories start. See the recipe in <a href="/en/docs/cb-budget-caps">set up a team with budget caps</a> and the vocabulary in <a href="/en/docs/credits" class="doc-term-def" data-def="The billing unit. 1 USD = 100 credits; usage converts at each model's list rate.">how credits work</a>.</p>

<p>Post-launch spikes are control problems as much as model problems (<a href="/en/blog/why-ai-bills-explode-after-go-live">five gaps</a>).</p>

<p>Projects that exceed allocation should surface as <strong>in debt</strong> until topped up—not as silent corporate bleed. Wallet vs team allocation: <a href="/en/docs/topup" class="doc-term-def" data-def="Adding credits to the wallet. Minimum 5 USD, pay as you go.">top up</a>.</p>
<h2 id="design-rules-for-caps-that-teams-accept">Design rules for caps that teams accept</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Alert before hard stop</strong> — page owners at 70–80% consumed.  </li>
<li><strong>Separate prod and experiment</strong> — different projects (<a href="/en/blog/one-project-one-key">one project, one key</a>).  </li>
<li><strong>Match model allowlists to budget</strong> — frontier only where allocation justifies it (<a href="/en/docs/models">models</a>).  </li>
<li><strong>Review weekly</strong> — <a href="/en/docs/spend">tracking spend</a> ranked by project.  </li>
<li><strong>Document 402</strong> — empty balance is a controlled failure (<a href="/en/docs/errors">errors</a>).</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="by-team-size-and-use-case">By team size and use case</h2>
<p><strong>Small:</strong> one production allocation + one sandbox.<br><strong>Mid:</strong> per product project caps; shared platform workspace.<br><strong>Enterprise:</strong> BU workspaces; quarterly reallocation; audit on Activity (<a href="/en/docs/monitoring">monitoring</a>).</p>
<p><strong>Agents:</strong> caps must assume <a href="/en/blog/what-is-the-agent-tax">agent tax</a>—task cost, not single-call cost.<br><strong>Batch:</strong> overnight jobs need explicit ceilings or they become the monthly story.</p>
<h2 id="one-week-rollout">One-week rollout</h2>
<p>Inventory unrestricted accounts → create projects → allocate → wire alerts → dual-run → revoke unlimited paths. Full governance loop: <a href="/en/blog/ai-governance-checklist">checklist</a>.</p>
<h2 id="a-more-modern-approach">A more modern approach</h2>
<p>Caps fail when enforcement is tribal knowledge. ATP Token treats project credit balance as the runtime ceiling and meters every request in credits under organization → workspace → project. Compatible with major SDK formats so caps attach without rewriting product code.</p>

<h2 id="faqs">FAQs</h2>
<h3 id="what-is-an-ai-spending-cap">What is an AI spending cap?</h3>
<p>An AI spending cap is a hard or soft limit on how much a team or project may spend on model usage in a period. Soft caps alert; hard caps stop or degrade traffic when the allocation is exhausted.</p>
<h3 id="where-should-ai-budget-limits-be-set">Where should AI budget limits be set?</h3>
<p>At the project or workload layer so one runaway system cannot consume the whole company budget. Org-level totals are for reporting; project allocations are for control.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-difference-between-allocated-and-consumed-credits">What is the difference between allocated and consumed credits?</h3>
<p>Allocated credits are pushed down to a child workspace or project as its ceiling. Consumed credits are what API calls actually spent. Watching both shows burn rate before the balance hits zero.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-stop-an-ai-bill-without-freezing-the-company">How do I stop an AI bill without freezing the company?</h3>
<p>Cap and pause the hot project first using project-scoped keys and allocations. Company-wide freezes are what you do when everything shares one key and one wallet.</p>
<h3 id="should-experimental-ai-work-share-production-budgets">Should experimental AI work share production budgets?</h3>
<p>No. Give experiments a separate project with a low allocation. Coding agents and sandboxes are common sources of uncapped burn.</p>
