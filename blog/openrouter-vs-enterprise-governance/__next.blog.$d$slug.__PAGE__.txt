<h2 id="faqs">FAQs</h2>
<h3 id="is-openrouter-an-enterprise-ai-governance-platform">Is OpenRouter an enterprise AI governance platform?</h3>
<p>OpenRouter is best understood as a multi-model routing and billing hub for developers. Enterprise governance platforms add organization hierarchy, project-scoped keys, formal budgets, and admin roles aimed at company-wide control.</p>
<h3 id="when-should-i-use-openrouter-vs-a-governance-gateway">When should I use OpenRouter vs a governance gateway?</h3>
<p>Use a routing hub when a small team needs fast access to many models. Move production multi-team workloads to a governance plane when finance needs attribution and security needs revoke and allowlists.</p>
<h3 id="can-openrouter-and-atp-token-be-used-together">Can OpenRouter and ATP Token be used together?</h3>
<p>Architecturally, research can stay on a routing hub while production sits on a governance gateway. What you should avoid is uncapped production traffic without project owners.</p>
<h3 id="what-does-atp-token-optimize-for-compared-to-routing-hubs">What does ATP Token optimize for compared to routing hubs?</h3>
<p>ATP Token optimizes for organization → workspace → project structure, credit allocation as caps, project model allowlists, and per-request audit with multi-format SDK compatibility.</p>
<h3 id="does-a-governance-platform-mean-fewer-models">Does a governance platform mean fewer models?</h3>
<p>It means models are explicitly allowed per project. The platform catalog can still be broad; the difference is permission, not curiosity alone.</p>
